A Word From Your Mentor

Two years in, and you chose to come back to page one on purpose. That's rarer than it sounds — most people who lose money trading just change the symbol and repeat the same mistake faster. Read this once now, and again in three months when you've forgotten it.

Every section in this notebook is about reading price — candles, structure, volume, gaps. None of it will save you if your size is wrong. Traders don't survive because they spot the best setups; they survive because they lose small enough, often enough, that one good trade erases five bad ones. Risk management isn't the boring chapter before the real trading starts. It is the trading.

Grade trades on process, not outcome. A trade you sized correctly, stopped correctly, and still lost is a good trade — journal it that way. A trade that made money because you moved your stop or skipped your checklist is a bad trade wearing a green number. Grade outcomes only, and you'll reinforce the exact habits that eventually blow up an account.

Demo will teach you mechanics — reading structure, drawing zones, running the Section 24 checklist until it's automatic. It won't teach you what it feels like to watch real money move against you. That's not a flaw in the exercise, just a limit to know going in: when you eventually go live, size so small it feels almost pointless. You're testing discipline under real emotion first, not trying to make money yet.

Last thing, before your first demo trade: open Section 26 and write one entry dated today that isn't a trade — a postmortem. What actually went wrong the first time around? Undersized stops, no plan, revenge trades after a loss? Be specific. Skip this and you risk relearning the chart perfectly while carrying forward the exact habit that ends an account.

01

Anatomy of a Candle

Every candle is a four-number argument between buyers and sellers, settled over one time period: open, high, low, close.

A candle isn't a shape — it's a compressed story. The body is the distance between open and close; the wicks (or shadows) are the high and low that price rejected before settling back inside the body. A long wick means price visited a level and got rejected from it hard. A tiny body with long wicks on both ends means neither side won — indecision.

Explain it like I'm 5

Think of a candle like a report card for one day of tug-of-war between Team Buyers and Team Sellers. The fat middle part shows who ended up winning and by how much. The thin lines poking out the top and bottom show how far each team got pulled before getting yanked back.

Sa Tagalog: Parang score card ng laban ng bumibili (buyer) at nagbebenta (seller) sa loob ng isang araw. Kung berde, nanalo yung bumibili. Kung pula, nanalo yung nagbenta. Yung manipis na linya sa taas at baba parang tug-of-war — may sandaling natulak sila palayo pero nabawi rin.

02

Candlestick Patterns Worth Knowing

A handful of shapes recur often enough to earn names. They mean nothing in isolation — context (trend, location, volume) is what makes them worth watching.

Explain it like I'm 5

These shapes are like facial expressions. A hammer looks like someone got knocked down and immediately bounced back up strong. A doji looks like a shrug — a tie, nobody won. The shape alone doesn't tell you the whole story, same as a face alone doesn't tell you what someone's really thinking.

Sa Tagalog: Parang facial expression ng market ang mga pattern na ito. Yung "hammer" parang natumba pero bumangon agad nang malakas. Yung "doji" parang balikat lang — walang nanalo, parang tabla (tie). Hindi porket may expression, alam mo na agad ang buong kwento.

Doji indecision

Open ≈ close, long wicks either side. Neither side controlled the period. Common at turning points, useless as a signal on its own.

Hammer reversal

Small body near the top, long lower wick, little/no upper wick. After a downtrend, shows sellers pushed price down and buyers slammed it back up.

Shooting Star reversal

Mirror of the hammer — small body near the bottom, long upper wick. After an uptrend, shows a failed push higher.

Bullish Engulfing reversal

A red candle fully swallowed by the next green candle's body. Momentum flipped hands within one period.

Bearish Engulfing reversal

Same idea, inverted — a green candle engulfed by the next red one. Often marks exhaustion at a high.

Inside Bar compression

A candle whose entire range sits inside the prior candle's range. Volatility is coiling — often precedes an expansion move.

03

Trend & Market Structure

Trend isn't a slope you eyeball — it's a sequence of highs and lows you can point to.

An uptrend is a series of higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL): every pullback finds support above the last one. A downtrend is the mirror — lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL). The market is only "trending" while that sequence keeps printing. The moment a higher-low fails to hold, or a lower-high gets broken, structure has shifted.

Explain it like I'm 5

An uptrend is like climbing a staircase — sometimes you step back down one stair, but you never go lower than the last time you stepped back, so overall you keep going up. A downtrend is walking down that same staircase — even a step up doesn't get you higher than before.

Sa Tagalog: Ang uptrend parang umaakyat ka ng hagdan — minsan bumababa ka ng isang baitang, pero hindi ka bumababa nang mas mababa sa huling pagbaba mo, kaya pataas pa rin overall. Ang downtrend, kabaliktaran — kahit tumaas ka ng konti, hindi mo pa rin naaabot yung dating pinakamataas.

BOS continuation

Break of Structure — price takes out the prior swing high (in an uptrend) confirming the trend is continuing.

CHoCH warning

Change of Character — the first higher-low or lower-high fails. Doesn't confirm reversal, but tells you to stop assuming continuation.

04

Support & Resistance

Think in zones, not lines. Price rarely respects a level to the exact tick.

Support is a price area where buying pressure has repeatedly overwhelmed selling; resistance is the reverse. The more times a zone has been tested, the more traders are watching it — which is exactly why zones eventually break: everyone's stop or order is sitting in the same place. When a resistance zone finally breaks, it frequently flips and becomes support on the retest — role reversal, one of the most reliable structural ideas in price action.

Explain it like I'm 5

Support is like a floor a ball keeps bouncing on. Resistance is like a ceiling the ball keeps bumping its head on. Here's the fun part: if the ball finally punches through the floor and falls to a new level, that same floor can turn into the ceiling if the ball tries to climb back up — like a trapdoor that only opens one way.

Sa Tagalog: Ang support parang sahig na tinatalbugan ng bola. Ang resistance parang kisame na binabangga ng bola paakyat. Kapag nabasag yung sahig at bumagsak yung bola sa mas mababa, pwedeng maging bagong kisame yung dating sahig kapag sumubok itong bumalik pataas — parang bitag na iisang direksyon lang ang bukas.
Field noteDraw zones with the wicks, not just the closes — the wick is where the market actually rejected. A zone drawn only from closing prices is often too narrow to mean anything on a retest.
05

Liquidity & Stop Hunts

Every obvious high or low has orders parked just beyond it — and price knows exactly where they're sitting.

When two swing highs land at roughly the same price, everyone can see it — which is exactly the problem. Traders who sold at that level have stop-losses resting just above it. Breakout traders have buy-stop orders resting just above it too, ready to trigger the moment price finally clears the level. That whole cluster of resting orders is called liquidity. Large players often need a lot of opposing orders to fill their own size without moving price too far against themselves — and a cluster of stops is exactly that. It's common for price to wick briefly through an obvious high or low, trigger that resting liquidity, and then reverse hard back the other way.

Explain it like I'm 5

Imagine a fisherman who knows exactly where all the fish like to gather, so he casts his net right there instead of randomly. Big traders do something similar: they know where everyone's "safety exit" orders are parked — just above an obvious high, just below an obvious low — so price gets pushed there on purpose to scoop those orders up before the real move happens.

Sa Tagalog: Isipin mo mangingisda na alam na alam kung saan nagtitipon ang mga isda, kaya doon niya inihahagis ang lambat imbes na kung saan-saan. Ganito rin ang ginagawa ng malalaking traders — alam nila kung saan nakatambak ang mga "safety exit" order ng mga tao, kaya sinasadyang idaan doon ang presyo para masagap muna iyon bago ang tunay na galaw.
Field noteThis is the same mechanic you've already met twice under different names — the Wyckoff Spring in Section 09 and the role-reversal retest in Section 04. "Liquidity grab" is just the name for what's actually happening underneath both: a brief move beyond an obvious level to collect the orders resting there.
06

Chart Patterns

Zoom out from a single candle to a whole shape, and price tends to repeat a handful of them — because the crowd behind the tape repeats the same behaviors.

These fall into three families. Reversal patterns (double top, head & shoulders, and their inverses) mark a trend running out of energy and turning the other way. Continuation patterns (flags, pennants, rectangles) are pauses — the market catching its breath mid-trend before carrying on in the same direction. Bilateral patterns (triangles) can break either way — the shape itself doesn't tell you which, only that volatility is compressing before a decision.

Explain it like I'm 5

A double top looks like the letter M and a double bottom looks like a W — price tried twice to break through and failed, twice. A head and shoulders looks exactly like it sounds: a bump, a bigger bump, a bump — three tries, with the middle one the strongest, before it gives up. A flag or triangle is the market pausing to catch its breath mid-run, like a runner slowing to a jog before sprinting again.

Sa Tagalog: Ang double top parang letrang M at ang double bottom parang W — dalawang beses sinubukan ng presyo tumawid pero nabigo nang dalawang beses. Ang head and shoulders literal na parang ulo at balikat — tatlong subok, yung gitna ang pinakamalakas, bago sumuko. Ang flag o triangle parang tumitigil lang sandali para huminga ang market habang tumatakbo, bago tumakbo ulit.
Field noteTreat the entry/stop/target lines drawn on both sheets as illustrations of where those three things tend to sit relative to the pattern — not fixed rules to copy onto your own chart. Your actual stop still comes from Section 04's zones (beyond the pattern's own high or low), and your position size still comes from the mentor letter's 1% math. A pattern tells you a shape formed; it doesn't set your risk for you.
07

Reading Volume

Price tells you what happened. Volume tells you how much conviction was behind it.

A breakout on heavy volume means real participation pushed price through a level — it's more likely to hold. A breakout on thin volume is easier to fade; there may not be enough behind it to sustain the move. Watch for two recurring tells: climax volume (an extreme volume spike after an extended move, often marking exhaustion rather than continuation) and declining volume on a pullback (a healthy sign in a trend — the retracement lacks conviction, so the prior move is likely to resume).

Explain it like I'm 5

Volume is like how loud the crowd cheers. A big move with a huge, loud cheer means tons of people really showed up and believe in it. A big move with barely any clapping means almost nobody showed up — it might not last.

Sa Tagalog: Ang volume parang lakas ng sigawan ng crowd. Kung malaking galaw ng presyo na may kasamang malakas na sigawan, ibig sabihin marami talaga sumali at naniniwala. Kung tahimik lang habang malaki yung galaw, baka konti lang sumali at hindi tatagal.
08

CME Gaps — Specific to Bitcoin Futures

This one matters directly for the chart you screenshotted: CME:BTC1! trades on exchange hours. Spot Bitcoin never sleeps.

CME Bitcoin futures only trade roughly Sunday 5pm CT through Friday 4pm CT, with a short daily maintenance break. Spot Bitcoin trades 24/7/365 on crypto exchanges. If spot price moves meaningfully over a weekend while CME is closed, the futures chart reopens Sunday evening at wherever spot now sits — leaving a visible empty space on the candlestick chart between Friday's close and Sunday's open. That empty space is the CME gap.

Explain it like I'm 5

Imagine a shop (CME) that closes on weekends, but the shop right next door (spot Bitcoin) never closes, ever. If a big sale happens next door over the weekend and the price changes a lot, when the first shop reopens Monday it has to suddenly jump to match — leaving a gap in its own price chart from where it left off Friday.

Sa Tagalog: Isipin mo tindahan (CME) na sarado tuwing weekend, pero yung katabi (spot Bitcoin) bukas talaga 24/7, walang tulog. Kung nagbago ang presyo doon habang sarado yung una, pagbukas nito ulit sa Lunes kailangan nitong tumalon papunta sa bagong presyo — kaya may puwang o "gap" sa chart nila mula noong huling sarado sila.
Field noteGap-fill is a tendency traders lean on, not a law of physics. Treat an open gap as a magnet worth marking on your chart — never as a trade signal by itself. Combine it with structure and volume before acting.
09

Spotting Accumulation

Big buyers can't fill a position in one candle without moving price against themselves — so they build it quietly, inside a range. Wyckoff's schematic is the classic map of what that looks like.

After a hard decline, "smart money" accumulation often follows a recognizable rhythm: a climactic sell-off, a snap-back rally, a sideways range where supply is absorbed, one final shakeout below the range to trigger stops and grab liquidity, and then a break upward on expanding volume.

Explain it like I'm 5

Imagine a whale wants to buy every mango at the market without the vendor noticing and raising the price. So the whale buys a little at a time, quietly, for weeks — the price just sits still. Right before leaving, the whale even pretends to walk away (scaring other buyers into selling cheap) so it can scoop up the last cheap mangoes — then finally lets everyone see it wants more, and the price shoots up.

Sa Tagalog: Isipin mo may "malaking pating" (big investor) na gustong bilhin lahat ng mangga sa palengke nang hindi napapansin ng tindera, para hindi tumaas agad ang presyo. Kaya dahan-dahan siyang bumibili nang tahimik habang nakatengga lang ang presyo. Bago umalis, nagkukunwari pa siyang aalis (para matakot yung iba at magbenta ng mura) — doon niya sinasagap yung natitira. Saka lang niya babatuk-batukin tumaas nang husto ang presyo.
PhaseWhat's happeningWhat to look for
ADowntrend exhaustsClimax volume, automatic rally, first test of the low
BRange builds ("the cause")Repeated tests of range edges, no follow-through either way
CFinal shakeoutSpring below range low on a wick, quickly reclaimed
DStrength showsBreak of range high on rising volume, shallow pullback (LPS)

Moving Averages & Bollinger Bands

Two overlays worth turning on from that indicator search dialog you screenshotted. Both are just math on top of price — the skill is knowing what the math is telling you.

A moving average (MA) is the average closing price over the last N candles, redrawn every candle so it "moves." It smooths out noise and shows you the trend without the zig-zag. MA20 (roughly one trading month on a daily chart) tracks the short-term trend and reacts fast. MA200 (roughly nine to ten months) is the slow one institutions watch as a line in the sand for the broader regime — price spending most of its time above MA200 is a structurally bullish market; below it, structurally bearish.

Field noteThe famous "Golden Cross" / "Death Cross" is usually built from the 50-day and 200-day MAs, not the 20 and 200 — a slower, less noisy pair. A 20/200 cross fires far more often and far earlier, which makes it faster but also far more prone to false signals. Know which pair you're looking at before you trust a cross.
Explain it like I'm 5

A moving average is like your average grade over your last 20 or 200 quizzes. One bad quiz doesn't crash it and one lucky quiz doesn't spike it — it moves slowly, so it shows you the real overall direction: are you actually improving, or actually slipping?

Sa Tagalog: Ang moving average parang average grades mo sa huling 20 o 200 quiz. Hindi basta babagsak o tataas dahil lang sa isang quiz — dahan-dahan ang galaw niya, kaya makikita mo yung totoong direksyon mo: tumataas ba talaga o bumababa ang grades mo overall.

Both MAs also work as dynamic support and resistance: in a healthy uptrend, pullbacks often stall right at the MA20 and bounce. The further price stretches away from an MA, the more "extended" it is — and the more likely a mean-reversion snap back toward it.

Bollinger Bands take this further: a middle band (usually the MA20), plus an upper and lower band drawn two standard deviations away from it. Since standard deviation measures how spread out price has been, the bands aren't fixed — they widen when volatility expands and squeeze tight when volatility dries up. A tight squeeze after a long quiet stretch is one of the more reliable warnings that a big directional move is loading, in either direction.

Explain it like I'm 5

Bollinger Bands are like a rubber band wrapped around a bouncy ball. When the ball is calm and barely moving, the rubber band sits tight and narrow. When the ball suddenly bounces wild and hard, the rubber band stretches wide open. A very tight, narrow rubber band after a calm stretch usually means a big bounce is coming soon.

Sa Tagalog: Ang Bollinger Bands parang rubber band sa paligid ng tumatalbog na bola. Kapag tahimik lang ang bola, masikip at makitid yung rubber band. Kapag biglang lumundag nang malakas, lumalawak yung rubber band. Kapag matagal nang masikip yung rubber band pagkatapos ng katahimikan, malapit na yatang lumundag nang malakas ang bola.

In a range mean-reversion

Price tagging the outer band with no trend behind it often snaps back toward the middle band (the MA20).

In a trend continuation

Price can "walk" the upper (or lower) band for many candles in a row. Treat repeated band touches as trend strength, not an automatic reversal signal.

Fibonacci Retracement & Extension

A pullback almost never retraces 100% of a move — and certain partial levels keep getting tested, because enough traders are watching the same ones.

Draw a line from a swing low to a swing high (in an uptrend) and a charting platform will divide that leg into ratios pulled from the Fibonacci sequence: 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, and 78.6%. These become horizontal levels where a pullback often stalls before the trend resumes — the 61.8% zone in particular is nicknamed the golden pocket. Beyond the original high, extension levels like 127.2% and 161.8% mark where the next leg might reasonably run out of steam — useful as a rough profit-target zone, not an exact number.

Explain it like I'm 5

Stretch a rubber band from a table up to your hand, then let a little tension out. It doesn't snap all the way back down to the table — it settles at a spot partway down. Fibonacci levels are the market's favorite "partway down" spots, and they work partly because so many traders are watching those exact same spots at the same time.

Sa Tagalog: Iunat mo yung rubber band mula sa mesa papunta sa kamay mo, tapos bitawan mo ng konti. Hindi ito babalik nang buo sa mesa — may parte kung saan ito huminto sa gitna. Ganyan din ang mga Fibonacci level — paboritong "paghinto sa gitna" ng market, at gumagana ito dahil marami ring traders ang nakatutok sa parehong mga parteng iyon.
Field noteFib levels are among the most self-fulfilling tools in this notebook — useful precisely because so many people draw the same lines, not because of any hidden law of markets. Treat a fib zone as a place to look for confirmation (a candle pattern, a volume shift, a structure retest) rather than a signal on its own.
12

Momentum: RSI & MACD

Price tells you where the market went. Momentum tells you how hard it's still trying to get there.

RSI (Relative Strength Index) compares the size of recent up-candles to recent down-candles and scales the result from 0 to 100. Above 70 is usually called overbought, below 30 oversold — but in a strong trend RSI can sit pinned near an extreme for a long stretch, so treat those zones as "worth watching," not an automatic signal. MACD plots the gap between a fast and a slow moving average (12 and 26 periods) against its own 9-period average — when the two cross, or when the whole thing crosses zero, momentum is shifting direction.

The single most useful thing either tool does is spot divergence: price making a new low while the oscillator makes a higher low. Price is still falling, but the force behind the fall is already weakening — often the earliest warning that a downtrend is running out of sellers (and the mirror version, at highs, warns a rally is running out of buyers).

Explain it like I'm 5

RSI is like a speedometer strapped to a runner — it tells you how tired and fast they're going, not how far they've run. Divergence is spotting a runner who's still moving forward but is clearly out of breath, wobbling, slowing down — a good sign they're about to stop, even though they haven't stopped yet.

Sa Tagalog: Ang RSI parang speedometer na nakakabit sa isang tumatakbo — sinasabi nito kung gaano siya kapagod at kabilis, hindi kung gaano na siya kalayo natakbo. Ang divergence ay ang pagkita sa isang tumatakbo pa rin pasulong pero halatang naubusan na ng hininga — senyales na malapit na siyang tumigil, kahit hindi pa siya tumitigil.
Field noteBoth indicators lag price — they're built from it, not ahead of it. "Overbought" in a strong uptrend can stay overbought for weeks. Use RSI and MACD to confirm what structure and volume are already telling you, not to fight an obvious trend on your own.
13

Timeframes & Top-Down Analysis

The same candle can be an entire trend on one timeframe and a single blip on another — always know which map you're holding.

Work top-down, not bottom-up. Start on a higher timeframe (Daily or Weekly) to read the dominant trend and mark the major support/resistance zones — this is your bias. Drop to a middle timeframe (4H or 1H) to see the current structure and whatever pattern is forming inside that bias. Only then drop to a lower timeframe (15m or 5m) to fine-tune exactly where you'd enter. The lower timeframe's job is timing, never overruling the direction the higher timeframe already set.

Explain it like I'm 5

It's like using Google Maps. You zoom out to see the whole country first so you know which direction you're generally headed. Then you zoom into the city, then the street, to find the exact door. Nobody uses a world map to parallel park — and nobody should use a 15-minute chart to decide whether the overall trend is up or down.

Sa Tagalog: Parang Google Maps — muna mong titingnan yung buong bansa (Daily) para malaman kung saang direksyon ka pupunta. Tapos zoom in ka sa siyudad (4H), tapos sa kalye (15-minuto) para mahanap yung eksaktong pinto. Walang gumagamit ng mapa ng buong mundo para lang mag-park ng sasakyan — at hindi dapat ginagamit ang 15-minutong chart para malaman kung pataas ba o pababa talaga ang market overall.
Field noteIf your 15-minute chart and your Daily chart disagree, the Daily wins the argument. A perfect-looking lower-timeframe setup against the higher-timeframe trend is a lower-probability trade, not a free pass to ignore the bigger picture.

Order Types & Futures Mechanics

Two different things worth knowing cold: the buttons you'll actually click, and what makes a continuous futures symbol like BTC1! behave the way it does.

Market Order

"Fill me right now, at whatever the current price is." Fastest way in or out — but on thin liquidity, your actual fill can slip worse than the price you saw.

Limit Order

"Only fill me at this price or better." You control the price, not the timing — it might never fill if the market never comes back to it.

Stop Order

"If price reaches this level, become a market order." The standard way to place a stop-loss or a breakout entry — can still slip in a fast move.

Stop-Limit

"If price reaches this level, place a limit order at this price." Protects against slippage, but in a fast move it can fail to fill at all.

Now the part specific to what you're actually charting: BTC1! isn't one single contract — it's a continuous contract, a stitched-together feed that always shows whichever quarterly CME Bitcoin futures contract is currently the front month, then automatically switches to the next one as expiration approaches. The real, tradable contracts underneath it expire quarterly (March, June, September, December) and are cash-settled — there's no actual bitcoin delivered.

Explain it like I'm 5

A market order is like buying a ticket from a scalper at whatever price they're asking, right now, because you need in immediately. A limit order is like haggling — you leave your offer and walk away, and they only call you if they accept it. A stop order is like a fire alarm wired to a sprinkler: the moment the trigger level is hit, it goes off automatically, no matter what else is happening. And BTC1! is like a relay race — it's not one runner the whole time, it's whoever is currently holding the baton, handed off every few months.

Sa Tagalog: Ang market order parang bibili ka ng ticket sa scalper kahit magkano na, dahil kailangan mo na talaga agad. Ang limit order parang pagtawad — iiwan mo lang yung alok mo at aalis, tatawagan ka lang nila kung tinanggap nila. Ang stop order parang alarm ng sunog na naka-kabit sa sprinkler — sa sandaling maabot ang trigger level, kusa itong gagalaw kahit ano pa ang nangyayari. At ang BTC1! parang relay race — hindi iisang tumatakbo mula umpisa hanggang wakas, kundi sinumang may hawak ng baton ngayon, na nagpapalit-palit tuwing ilang buwan.
Field noteIf you ever place a real order (not just study the chart), check which specific expiration month you're actually trading — the continuous BTC1! chart is for reading history, not for clicking buttons on.

Explain it like I'm 5

Scalping is table tennis — lightning fast, tiny points, nonstop. Day trading is a basketball game — quick-paced but it ends by the final buzzer, nothing carried into tomorrow. Swing trading is a boxing match with several rounds spread over days — you wait for your opening. Position trading is a marathon — months of patience for one big result.

Sa Tagalog: Ang scalping parang table tennis — sobrang bilis, maliliit na puntos, walang tigil. Ang day trading parang laro ng basketball — mabilis pero may buzzer sa dulo, walang dala sa susunod na araw. Ang swing trading parang boxing match na maraming rounds sa loob ng ilang araw — naghihintay ka ng tamang pagkakataon. Ang position trading parang marathon — buwan-buwang pasensya para sa isang malaking resulta.
Field notePick the style that matches your actual schedule, not the one that looks most exciting online. A swing trader with a 9-to-5 job staring at 1-minute charts on lunch break is set up to make exhausted, emotional decisions in a style that was never built for that little attention.
16

Trading Psychology

The chart doesn't move your account balance. Your reaction to the chart does.

Every rule in the "Before You Start" letter exists to fight one of a handful of predictable failure modes. Here they are by name, so you can catch yourself mid-mistake instead of only in the postmortem.

FOMO

Jumping into a move that already ran, without a plan, purely because you're afraid of missing it. Fix: if you missed the setup, you missed the trade — there is always another one.

Revenge Trading

Trying to immediately win back a loss with a bigger, less-planned trade. Fix: the "two losses ends the day" rule exists specifically for this.

Overconfidence

A win streak convincing you the rules don't apply right now, so size creeps up and the plan gets skipped. Fix: grade every trade by process, win or lose, so a streak can't rewrite your rules.

Confirmation Bias

Only noticing the chart details that agree with the trade you already wanted to take. Fix: the Section 24 checklist exists to make you check the boxes that could prove you wrong too.

Sunk-Cost Holding

Refusing to close a loser because "it'll come back," moving the stop further instead of admitting the plan failed. Fix: the stop was set by a calmer version of you, before the trade — trust that version, not the one currently staring at red numbers.

Explain it like I'm 5

Trading feelings are a rollercoaster — from "best day ever" to "worst day ever" in the space of a few candles, before you've had any practice controlling that ride. The market doesn't know or care how you feel. But if you let your feelings drive, they will absolutely hijack a perfectly good plan.

Sa Tagalog: Ang emosyon sa pagtrade parang rollercoaster — mula sa "pinakamasayang araw" hanggang "pinakamalungkot na araw" sa loob lang ng ilang candle, bago ka pa man masanay kontrolin ito. Walang pakialam ang market sa nararamdaman mo. Pero kung hahayaan mong ang emosyon ang mangunot, ito mismo ang sisira sa magandang plano mo.
Field noteNotice which of these five you personally lean toward — most traders have one or two favorites. Write it at the top of your journal (Section 26) and check for it specifically, the same way you'd check a blind spot before changing lanes.

Position Sizing & Risk of Ruin

A profitable strategy can still bust an account if the bet size is wrong. This is the math the "1% rule" in the mentor letter is actually protecting you from.

Even a strategy with a real edge will lose several trades in a row sometimes — that's normal, not a sign something's broken. What matters is whether your account can survive that losing streak. Risking a small, fixed percentage per trade means a bad stretch dents the account; risking a large percentage means the same stretch can wipe it out, even if the strategy itself was fine the whole time.

Losses and the gains needed to recover from them aren't symmetric, either — the deeper the hole, the disproportionately bigger the climb back out:

Think of your account like a stack of poker chips you brought for the whole night, not just one hand. Betting a tiny slice each hand means a bad run of luck still leaves you chips to keep playing. Betting a huge slice each hand means one bad run and you're out of the game entirely — even if you were actually the better player.

Leverage & Margin

Futures are traded on margin by default — a small deposit controls a much bigger position. That cuts both ways, always.

Leverage means a small amount of your own money (the margin) controls a much larger position. It doesn't create profit out of nothing — it multiplies whatever the underlying price move already was, gains and losses alike. Maintenance margin is the minimum equity your broker requires you to keep in the position; fall below it and you get a margin call (add funds or the position gets force-closed) or outright liquidation (the position is closed for you, often at a worse price than you'd have chosen).

Explain it like I'm 5

Leverage is a magnifying glass pointed at whatever already happened to the price — it doesn't invent extra money, it just makes the real result look and feel much bigger, good or bad. A margin call is like a loan officer calling to say your collateral isn't covering the loan anymore, and if you don't add more right now, they'll take back what's theirs.

Sa Tagalog: Ang leverage parang magnifying glass na nakatutok sa anumang nangyari na sa presyo — hindi ito gumagawa ng extra pera, pinapalaki lang nito ang totoong resulta, maganda man o masama. Ang margin call parang tumatawag na loan officer na nagsasabing hindi na sapat ang collateral mo sa utang — kung hindi ka magdadagdag agad, babawiin nila ang sa kanila.
Field noteHigher leverage means your stop-loss distance (in price) has to be tighter for the same dollar risk from the mentor letter's math — it doesn't let you skip that math, it makes getting it wrong more expensive, faster.

VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price)

The average price everyone actually traded at today, weighted by how much volume happened at each level — not just a plain average of the high and low.

VWAP resets at the start of each session and updates candle by candle, weighting each price by how much volume traded there. Institutions often use it as a fairness benchmark — trying to buy below VWAP or sell above it means you got a better-than-average deal for the session. As a read: price consistently above VWAP suggests buyers are in control for the day so far; consistently below suggests sellers are.

Explain it like I'm 5

VWAP is like figuring out the average price everyone paid for gas today at one station, weighted by how many people bought at each price — not just averaging the morning price and the evening price. It tells you whether the price you paid was a good deal or a bad deal compared to everyone else today.

Sa Tagalog: Ang VWAP parang paghahanap ng average presyong binayaran ng lahat ng bumili ng gas ngayong araw sa istasyong iyon, batay sa dami ng bumili sa bawat presyo — hindi lang basic average ng presyo sa umaga at hapon. Sinasabi nito kung mura o mahal ang binili mo kumpara sa lahat ng ibang bumili ngayong araw.
Field noteVWAP is an intraday tool by design — it resets every session, so it means the most on lower timeframes (5m–1H). On the Daily chart you've been practicing on, MA20 from Section 10 is doing a similar job.
20

Order Book & Bid-Ask Spread

Behind every candle is a live list of everyone's current offers — the order book is where that list lives.

The order book shows resting bids (buy orders) below the current price and resting asks or offers (sell orders) above it. The highest bid and the lowest ask define the spread — the small gap you effectively pay to trade immediately with a market order instead of waiting with a limit order. A "thick" book with large size stacked near the current price is liquid and harder to move; a "thin" book can let a modest order jump price several ticks.

Explain it like I'm 5

The order book is like a whiteboard at a garage sale listing everyone's current offers — buyers write what they're willing to pay, the seller writes what they're asking. The spread is the gap between the highest offer to buy and the lowest asking price to sell — the little bit of wiggle room in the middle.

Sa Tagalog: Ang order book parang blackboard sa garage sale na nakalista ang lahat ng alok — sinusulat ng mga bibili kung magkano ang kaya nilang bayaran, at sinusulat ng nagbebenta kung magkano ang hinihingi niya. Ang spread ay ang agwat sa pagitan ng pinakamataas na alok bumili at pinakamababang hinihinging presyo — ang bahagyang puwang sa gitna.
Field noteA wider-than-usual spread is itself information — it often shows up right before or during high-impact news (Section 23), when market makers pull back because they don't want to get caught quoting a stale price.

Expectancy: Do You Have an Edge?

Win rate alone tells you almost nothing. What matters is win rate multiplied by how big your average win and loss actually are.

Expectancy per trade, in units of risk (R): (Win% × Avg Win) − (Loss% × Avg Loss). A system that wins less than half the time can still be strongly profitable if its winners are big enough relative to its losers — and a system that wins most of the time can quietly lose money if its rare losses are large enough.

Explain it like I'm 5

Think of it like a carnival game, not a coin flip. You don't need to win every round — you need the prize you win to be worth more than the cost of the rounds you lose. A game you win rarely but with a huge prize can beat a game you win constantly but with a tiny prize each time.

Sa Tagalog: Isipin mo parang laro sa karnabal, hindi coin flip. Hindi mo kailangang manalo sa bawat round — kailangan lang mas malaki ang halaga ng premyo kumpara sa gastos mo sa mga round na natalo ka. Ang larong bihira mo manalo pero malaki ang premyo ay pwedeng mas mahusay kaysa sa larong madalas mo manalo pero konti lang ang premyo bawat beses.
Field noteYour demo journal (Section 26) is how you actually measure your own numbers instead of guessing them. After enough logged trades, compute your real win rate and average R — that's your real edge, not the one you assumed you had.

Macro Context & Correlation

BTC1! doesn't trade in a vacuum — the watchlist panel you started with (SPX, NDQ, DJI, VIX, DXY) sitting right above your chart wasn't decoration.

Since institutional futures adoption, Bitcoin has increasingly traded like a high-beta risk asset alongside stocks — often rising and falling with the Nasdaq/S&P during broad "risk-on" and "risk-off" swings. DXY (the US Dollar Index) frequently moves opposite to risk assets: a strong dollar (risk-off) often coincides with BTC weakness, a weak dollar (risk-on) often coincides with BTC strength. VIX, the "fear index," spiking usually means broad de-risking across markets — BTC included, despite its "digital gold" reputation not always holding up in practice during those moments.

Explain it like I'm 5

Checking the wider market before trading BTC is like checking the regional weather forecast before heading to the beach, not just looking at the one patch of sky above your house. If it's stormy everywhere around you, your sunny little patch of sky might not stay sunny for long.

Sa Tagalog: Ang pagtingin sa buong market bago mag-trade ng BTC ay parang pagtingin muna sa regional weather forecast bago pumunta sa beach, hindi lang yung ulap sa ibabaw mismo ng bahay mo. Kung maulan sa buong paligid, baka hindi rin magtagal ang maaraw na bahagi ng langit sa itaas mo.
Field noteYou don't need to trade DXY or VIX to benefit from watching them — a quick glance at the same watchlist panel you already have open is enough context before trusting a setup on BTC1! alone.

Elliott Wave Theory

A trend rarely moves in one straight push — it tends to unfold in a repeating rhythm of five waves forward, three waves back.

The theory: a trend advances in five waves (1–2–3–4–5), where waves 1, 3, and 5 move with the trend and waves 2 and 4 are corrective pullbacks against it — wave 3 is typically the longest and strongest. That five-wave move is then followed by a three-wave correction (A–B–C) against the whole thing, before the next five-wave sequence can begin. Two loose rules traders lean on: wave 2 shouldn't retrace past the very start of wave 1, and wave 4 usually shouldn't trade back into wave 1's price territory.

Explain it like I'm 5

It's like watching ocean waves roll in: a big wave forward, a little pull back, another big wave forward, a smaller pull back, one more big push — then a rest of three smaller waves before the next big set arrives. Nature repeats this same rhythm at different sizes — a small trend often looks like a tiny copy of a bigger one.

Sa Tagalog: Parang panonood ng mga alon sa dagat — malaking alon pasulong, konting atras, malaking alon ulit, mas maliit na atras, isa pang malakas na tulak — tapos may pahingang tatlong maliliit na alon bago dumating ang susunod na malaking hanay. Umuulit ang kalikasan ng ritmong ito sa iba't ibang sukat — madalas ang isang maliit na trend ay parang maliit na kopya lang ng mas malaking trend.
Field noteTen different traders can count ten different waves on the same chart in real time — the count is usually only obvious after the fact. Use Elliott Wave to think about rhythm and where a move might be tiring, never as a precise entry trigger by itself.

Harmonic Patterns

A more precise cousin of the Fibonacci tool from Section 11 — patterns built from exact, measured ratios instead of a general shape.

Harmonic patterns (Gartley, Bat, Butterfly, Crab, and others) map a five-point swing labeled X-A-B-C-D, where each leg must retrace or extend the previous one by a specific Fibonacci ratio. A classic Gartley, for example, wants point B to retrace close to 61.8% of the X-A leg, and point D to complete around a 78.6% retracement of that same leg — where several of those ratios converge is called the PRZ, the Potential Reversal Zone.

Explain it like I'm 5

Regular chart patterns are like sketching a shape freehand. Harmonic patterns are like a dance with an exact, measured choreography — every step has to land within a specific percentage, not just "roughly about there."

Sa Tagalog: Ang regular na chart pattern ay parang libreng pagguhit ng hugis. Ang harmonic pattern ay parang sayaw na may eksaktong sukat sa bawat hakbang — kailangang tumapat sa partikular na porsyento ang bawat parte, hindi lang basta-basta "malapit na."
Field noteHarmonic patterns are precise enough that most traders use dedicated scanning tools rather than drawing them by eye. Treat this section as knowing the vocabulary and the idea behind it, not a requirement to spot one manually.

Trading Sessions & Best Times to Trade

Crypto trades around the clock, but activity isn't spread evenly across the day — some hours are the mall at closing time, others are the mall on Black Friday.

Three broad sessions drive global volume: Asian, London, and New York. Volatility tends to pick up at each session's open and especially during the London/New York overlap, when both major regions are active at once. The quiet stretch in between — late in the US afternoon into the early Asian session — often produces thinner, choppier price action that's easy to misread as a real move.

Explain it like I'm 5

The market is technically always open, but it's not always busy. Trading during the dead hours is like walking into an empty mall — nothing much is happening and any noise stands out too much. Trading during the big overlap is like the mall on its biggest sale day — way more real activity to actually read.

Sa Tagalog: Bukas palagi ang market pero hindi laging maingay. Ang magtrade sa oras na tulog lahat ay parang pagpasok sa walang taong mall — kaunti lang ang nangyayari at kahit maliit na ingay, lumalaki. Ang magtrade sa oras ng overlap ay parang pagpunta sa mall sa pinakamalaking sale — mas maraming tunay na galaw na pwedeng basahin.
Field noteThis connects directly to Section 08's CME gaps — the quietest stretch of all is the CME weekend closure itself, when only spot crypto is even moving.

Confluence: Stacking Your Edges

No single tool in this notebook is reliable by itself. The real signal shows up when several of them agree, at the same price, without you forcing it.

Confluence is what it's called when independent tools — a support zone, a Fibonacci level, a moving average, a volume signature, a candle pattern — all point to the same price area at the same time. None of them is magic on its own; what makes a zone worth real attention is several unrelated methods landing in the same place without you having to stretch to make them agree.

Explain it like I'm 5

It's like asking four different friends the same question separately, without letting them talk to each other first. If all four happen to give you the same answer anyway, that means a lot more than if you'd only asked one friend.

Sa Tagalog: Parang paghingi ng opinyon sa apat na magkaibang kaibigan nang hiwa-hiwalay, hindi sila nag-uusap sa isa't isa. Kung parehong-pareho ang sagot nilang apat, mas malaki ang ibig sabihin niyan kaysa kung isa lang kaibigan ang tinanong mo.
Field noteThis is exactly what the Section 24 checklist is for — it's not a formality, it's how you check for confluence on purpose instead of noticing it by accident. The more boxes genuinely check out at the same price, the more that zone earned your attention.

Glossary

Quick reference for terms used above.

OHLC
Open, High, Low, Close — the four prices that define one candle.
BOS (Break of Structure)
Price takes out the prior swing high/low in the direction of the trend, confirming continuation.
CHoCH (Change of Character)
The first swing that breaks the established HH/HL or LH/LL sequence — an early warning of possible reversal.
Liquidity
Clusters of resting stop-loss and pending orders, often just beyond an obvious swing high/low — a common magnet for price.
Spring
A brief false breakdown below a trading range that quickly reclaims the range, trapping short sellers before markup.
Upthrust
The distribution mirror of a spring — a false breakout above a range that fails and reverses down.
CME Gap
An empty space on the CME Bitcoin futures chart where price jumped between Friday's close and the next session's open because spot moved while futures were closed.
Role Reversal
A broken resistance zone acting as new support on retest, or vice versa.
R:R (Risk:Reward)
Distance from entry to target, divided by distance from entry to stop — how much you stand to make versus lose.
MA20 / MA200
Average closing price over the last 20 or 200 candles. MA20 tracks short-term trend; MA200 marks the broader bullish/bearish regime.
Golden Cross / Death Cross
A faster MA crossing above (golden) or below (death) a slower MA — classically the 50-day over the 200-day.
Bollinger Bands
A moving average (basis) plus an upper and lower band drawn two standard deviations away, widening with volatility and squeezing when it dries up.
Head & Shoulders
Three swing highs, the middle one tallest, sitting on a "neckline" — a classic reversal pattern. Upside-down, it's an Inverse Head & Shoulders (bullish).
Double Top / Bottom
Two failed attempts at the same high (top, bearish) or same low (bottom, bullish) — shaped like an M or a W.
Flag / Pennant
A brief, tight sideways or counter-trend pause after a strong move, usually resolving in the direction of that original move.
Marubozu
A candle with no wicks at all, or almost none — one side had full control from open to close, no rejection either way.
Harami
A small candle sitting entirely inside the prior candle's body — the mirror-image cousin of an engulfing pattern, showing momentum stalling rather than reversing outright.
Three White Soldiers / Three Black Crows
Three consecutive strong candles in the same direction with little overlap — a forceful, sustained push by one side.
Liquidity
Clusters of resting stop-loss and pending orders, often just beyond an obvious swing high/low — a common magnet for price.
Fibonacci Retracement
Horizontal levels (23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%) dividing a price swing, marking where a pullback often stalls before the trend resumes.
RSI (Relative Strength Index)
A 0–100 oscillator comparing recent up-moves to down-moves; above 70 is "overbought," below 30 "oversold."
MACD
The gap between a fast and slow moving average, plotted against its own signal line — crossovers flag a shift in momentum.
Divergence
Price makes a new high/low while an oscillator (RSI/MACD) doesn't confirm it — a warning that the move's underlying momentum is weakening.
Market / Limit / Stop Order
Market fills immediately at the current price; Limit only fills at a chosen price or better; Stop becomes a market order once a trigger price is hit.
Contract Rollover
The point where a continuous futures symbol (like BTC1!) switches its data feed from an expiring contract to the next quarterly one.
FOMO
Fear Of Missing Out — entering a trade late, without a plan, purely because the move already looks like it's happening without you.
Margin / Leverage
Margin is the collateral you put up; leverage is the multiple of exposure that collateral controls. Both gains and losses scale with the multiple.
Margin Call / Liquidation
A demand for more collateral when a leveraged position's equity falls too low; liquidation is the position being force-closed when it isn't met.
VWAP
Volume-Weighted Average Price — the session's average traded price, weighted by volume at each level; resets every session.
Bid / Ask / Spread
The bid is the highest resting buy order, the ask the lowest resting sell order; the spread is the gap between them.
Expectancy
(Win% × Avg Win) − (Loss% × Avg Loss) — the actual measure of whether a strategy is profitable over many trades, not win rate alone.
Risk-On / Risk-Off
Market-wide moods where money flows into riskier assets (risk-on) or out of them into safety (risk-off), typically moving stocks, BTC, and the US dollar together or in opposition.
Elliott Wave
A theory that trends unfold in a five-wave impulse (with the trend) followed by a three-wave correction (against it), repeating at different scales.
Harmonic Pattern
A precise XABCD price structure where each leg matches a specific Fibonacci ratio, pointing to a Potential Reversal Zone (PRZ).
Trading Session
One of the three broad global trading windows — Asian, London, New York — whose overlaps typically bring the highest volume and volatility.
Confluence
Multiple independent tools (a zone, a fib level, an average, a volume signature) all pointing to the same price area at once — the basis for a higher-conviction read.
