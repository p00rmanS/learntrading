Understanding Candlestick Charts

Candlestick charts are the most popular way to display price action in trading. Each candle represents a specific time period (1 minute, 5 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day, etc.) and shows four key prices:

📌 The 4 Key Prices:
  • Open: The first price when the period opens
  • Close: The last price when the period closes
  • High: The highest price during the period
  • Low: The lowest price during the period

Candle Anatomy

Bullish Candle

Price closed higher than it opened. Green indicates bullish sentiment.

Bearish Candle

Price closed lower than it opened. Red indicates bearish sentiment.

Key Components:
  • Body (Real Body): The rectangular part showing the distance between Open and Close
  • Wicks (Shadows): The thin lines showing the High and Low prices
  • Color: Usually green/white for bullish, red/black for bearish

Common Candlestick Patterns

Hammer
Small body at top, long lower wick. Signal of reversal after downtrend.
Shooting Star
Small body at bottom, long upper wick. Signal of potential reversal from uptrend.
Doji
Open and close nearly equal, creating a cross. Shows indecision in the market.
Engulfing
Larger candle fully contains the previous candle's body. Signal reversal.

Support & Resistance Levels

Support and Resistance are fundamental concepts in technical analysis. They represent price levels where the asset historically struggles to break past.

📌 What You Need to Know:
  • Support: A price level where the asset tends to stop falling and bounce back up (floor)
  • Resistance: A price level where the asset tends to stop rising and fall back down (ceiling)

Why Do They Matter?

  • Traders use these levels to identify entry and exit points
  • They help predict potential price movements
  • More times a price touches a level, the stronger that level becomes
  • When support breaks, it often becomes new resistance (and vice versa)

Real-World Example

Imagine a stock bounces off the $50 level 3 times without breaking below. This $50 level becomes a strong support.

If the price breaks below $50, traders watch for a potential new support lower. Meanwhile, $50 becomes potential resistance on bounces back up.

Breakouts: Understanding Price Breakthroughs

A breakout occurs when price breaks through an established support or resistance level with conviction, often signaling the beginning of a new trend.

📌 Breakout Definition:
  • Price moves above resistance (bullish breakout)
  • Price moves below support (bearish breakout)
  • Usually accompanied by high volume
  • Often marks the start of a new trend

Types of Breakouts

Bullish Breakout

↗️ Price breaks ABOVE Resistance

Signals uptrend. Traders often enter LONG positions.

Bearish Breakout

↙️ Price breaks BELOW Support

Signals downtrend. Traders often enter SHORT positions.

Breakout Visualization

Key Breakout Signals

  • Volume Surge: Strong breakouts are confirmed by high trading volume
  • Clean Break: Price should close decisively through the level
  • Pullback: After breakout, price often "retests" the level before continuing
  • Risk: False breakouts (fakeouts) can trap traders - always use stop losses
